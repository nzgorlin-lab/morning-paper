The study highlights the importance of using accurate measures to track children's health and development. The research was conducted by a team of scientists who analyzed data from a large cohort of children.

The study's findings have significant implications for our understanding of childhood obesity and development. The researchers note that the traditional measure of BMI is not always accurate, and that a more nuanced approach is needed to understand the complex changes that occur in children's bodies during this period. The study's results suggest that children's body composition is still changing, even if their BMI returns to earlier levels. This has important implications for public health policy and interventions aimed at preventing childhood obesity.
